# F17.9 — Time as a budget: the fit (C7, part b)

> The second half of §1 of [`f17-followups.md`](./f17-followups.md). [`F17.8`](./f17.8.md) priced the
> instrument and showed an author the arithmetic; this makes the planner obey it. Splitting them was
> deliberate — an author got to check the numbers against their own expectations for a release before
> anything started dropping topics on the strength of them.

## What was wrong

`sessionBudgetSeconds` was a number an author could read and nothing else. An instrument set to ten
minutes planned exactly as one set to no budget at all: the only bound the planner actually enforced
was `maxConditionalTopics`, a count, which cannot see that one topic is ten likerts and another is
three.

A setting that describes an intention and changes no behaviour is worse than no setting. The author
believes the interview is bounded; it is not.

## Confirmed decisions

- **No budget means no fit stage.** `applyGuardrails` takes an optional `budget`; absent — the
  default, and therefore nearly every version — the plan is byte-for-byte what the same inputs
  produced before this shipped. Enforcement that switched itself on would change what every existing
  instrument asks.

- **A rule-seated topic is never dropped.** An author's "always ask about compliance" is not a
  preference the arithmetic gets to overrule. If the rules alone bust the budget the interview runs
  long — and the settings tab already reports that configuration as an error (`budget_below_floor`).
  This is the one case where a plan may exceed its budget.

- **The lowest-ranked goes first**, meaning the last thing seated: the planner's own least confident
  pick, or the last fallback. The model ordered them, so the budget takes them back in reverse rather
  than inventing a second ranking of its own.

- **The blind-spot check's seconds are reserved, not free.** It is chosen from what did _not_ make
  the cut, so what it costs depends on what the fit drops — which is why the reserve is re-asked on
  every pass rather than computed once. This is exactly the omission `f17-followups.md` §1 records in
  the client's own arithmetic (their worst-case plan comes to 348s against a 334s allowance), and
  the reason the fit is seated **before** the check rather than after it.

- **The fit runs after the fallback**, not only after the rules. A safety net that could seat an
  interview nobody has time for is a safety net with a hole in it.

- **Drop, never downgrade.** A topic that does not fit is removed, not silently re-seated at `light`.
  A `light` topic is a sample, and quietly converting a scored area into a sampled one would change
  what a report is entitled to claim about a respondent — the distinction `NotAssessedTopic.partial`
  exists to protect. Arbitrary-granularity injection is C6, and its own decision.

- **An unpriced topic costs nothing.** No entry in the cost map means the topic resolves to no
  members. Charging a guess for it would drop a real topic to make room for an imaginary one.

- **A dropped topic is recorded as `source: 'budget'`**, a new `ScopeDecisionSource` that only ever
  appears on an exclusion. "There was no time for this" points an author at the setting; "the agent
  did not pick it" points them at the criteria. Only one of them is true, and the admin surface is
  where that difference gets acted on.

- **The plan carries its own arithmetic.** `budgetSeconds` / `estimatedSeconds` are written onto the
  plan and recomputed by nobody. The instrument can be edited afterwards, so a figure derived from
  today's questions would answer "what would this interview cost now" — not the question an admin
  holding a challenged report is asking. Both are omitted (never `0`) when there was no budget, so a
  plan that predates budgets and a plan fitted to nothing do not read the same.

- **An amendment still overrides it.** A respondent who asks to be covered on something gets it at
  `full` depth whatever the budget says — the same way amendment already overrides the topic count,
  and for the same reason: they are the one spending the time.

**Tests** — 13 new cases in `guardrails.test.ts` covering the fit (costs handed in directly, so each
case states its own arithmetic rather than making a reader add up likerts): the no-budget default,
dropping in reverse rank, a cheap fourth topic fitting where an expensive one did not, the rule
exemption, the fallback being fitted too, the check-topic reserve, the recorded reason, the
floor-exceeds-budget case, and the unpriced topic. `budget.test.ts` gains `plannedSeconds`,
`matrixRowCount`, and — the one that ties the two halves together — the existing worked example run
through `applyGuardrails`, where the third expensive topic is dropped precisely to pay for the
blind-spot check. `plan-scope.test.ts` gains the wiring cases: a priced version reaches the planner
with real costs, a matrix is priced per row through the trigger, and a version with no budget makes
no extra queries.

## What this does not do

- **It does not shorten an interview that is already running.** The plan is decided once, at the end
  of the opening, and frozen. A respondent who takes four minutes over a question the model priced at
  45 seconds is not re-planned — the budget bounds what is _asked_, not what is _spent_.
- **It does not price the conversation.** Only items. The interviewer's own turns, clarifications and
  probes cost the respondent time that no setting here accounts for. G03 ("one probe maximum across
  the opening", §5 of the follow-ups) is the piece that would bound that, and it is still open.
