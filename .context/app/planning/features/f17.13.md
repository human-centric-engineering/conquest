# F17.13 — cohort statistics over comparably-assessed respondents

> §6 of [`f17-followups.md`](./f17-followups.md), and the only item on that list that produced a
> **wrong answer** rather than a thin one.

## What was wrong

F17.5 put `assessedItemCount` / `totalItemCount` on every `ScaleScore` and shipped
`isPartiallyAssessed()` so that every surface rendering a band could ask the question. The cohort
dataset never asked it.

`buildScoring`'s `collect()` pushed every session's `raw` and `band` into the same arrays. A
respondent scored on three of a scale's eight items sat in the same mean, and the same band column,
as one scored on all eight — and the chart looked exactly as confident either way. Under
`method: 'sum'` the narrowed respondent also scores systematically lower, so the direction of the
error depends on the schema rather than on anything about the cohort.

A cohort report over an adaptive instrument was comparing respondents who answered different
instruments.

## Confirmed decisions

- **Exclude, do not flag.** A flagged-but-included score still moves the mean, and a reader who sees
  a mean does not go looking for a footnote before believing it. The mean has to be a statement
  about one instrument.

- **Report the exclusion.** `partiallyAssessed` rides beside `respondents` on every scale summary
  and every segment value. An aggregate that quietly discards respondents is its own kind of
  dishonesty, and this count is the reader's only signal that the instrument narrowed underneath the
  chart. `0` for every non-adaptive version, which is most of them.

- **"Not reportable" is not "too few respondents".** When a scale was partial for everyone,
  `respondents` falls to 0 and the digest says so in its own words. k-anonymity's suppression means
  "we have the number and will not show it"; this means "nobody was measured on this scale as
  authored". A bigger cohort fixes one and not the other, and a writer told the wrong one would
  offer the wrong caveat.

- **Silence beats a confident wrong number.** Where exclusion drops a scale below the k-anonymity
  floor, it suppresses — the same as any small sample. That is the correct direction: the previous
  behaviour was a precise figure describing nobody.

- **A pre-P17 score row still counts.** `isPartiallyAssessed` reads absent counts as "everything was
  asked", which is what was true when those rows were written. A retrospective exclusion would empty
  every historical chart to no one's benefit.

- **`respondents` changes meaning, deliberately.** It now counts respondents the figure is _about_.
  The k-anonymity floor applies to that count, which is the population being disclosed.

## What shipped

| File                                             | Change                                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `lib/app/questionnaire/cohort-report/dataset.ts` | `collect()` skips partially-assessed scores and counts them; both call sites carry the count through                    |
| `lib/app/questionnaire/cohort-report/types.ts`   | `partiallyAssessed` on `CohortScaleSummary` and `CohortScaleSegmentValue`; `respondents` re-documented                  |
| `lib/app/questionnaire/cohort-report/content.ts` | The writer's digest states the exclusion per scale and per dimension, and distinguishes "not reportable" from "too few" |

**Tests** — three cases in `dataset.test.ts`: a narrowed respondent excluded from both the mean and
the band counts (six at 3.0 and one at 9.0 must produce 3.0, not 3.86); a scale partial for everyone
reporting nothing rather than a confident wrong number; and a pre-P17 score row still counting.
Three in `content.test.ts` for the digest wording, including that a scale line never borrows
k-anonymity's "too few".

## Still open

The **admin cohort surfaces do not render `partiallyAssessed` yet** — nothing outside the report
digest reads `dataset.scoring` today (the chart specs are over questions and data slots, not
scales), so there is no chart to annotate. The moment a scale chart is added, it must print the
count; the field is there so that surface has no excuse.
