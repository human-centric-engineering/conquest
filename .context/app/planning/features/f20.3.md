# F20.3 — Reading the answer, in parallel

> Three model calls read what the respondent just said, one after another, for no reason other than
> the order they were written in. Two of them are now concurrent. The third stays where it is, and
> the reason is the interesting part.

## What was wrong

[`F20.1`](./f20.1.md) measured the turn: extraction ~879 ms, sensitivity detection ~279 ms,
seriousness judge ~371 ms — **~1.53 s of an ~5.1 s turn**, run strictly in sequence. None of the
three reads another's output.

## What changed (A1)

`extractAnswers` and `detectSensitivity` now run together, in both the question-mode and data-slot
orchestrators.

The safety of it rests on one fact: **neither call reads the other's result.** Both take only
`state` and the message, and `mergeSensitivitySignals` combines the extractor's own `sensitivity`
field, the dedicated detector's verdict and the deterministic keyword floor afterwards — strongest
signal wins — whichever order they land in. The seriousness gate below reads only that merged
result, so it is still strictly downstream of everything that could protect a disclosure.

No input, no prompt and no verdict changed. Projected saving on the F20.1 sample: **~279 ms/turn**.

Two details that are not obvious:

- **`toolCalls` are still recorded in the original order.** They are a read surface — Diagnostics,
  the persisted turn row — and letting completion order decide them would make the record
  non-deterministic between two otherwise identical turns. The calls are launched together and
  recorded in sequence afterwards.
- **The detector is still opt-in.** Concurrency must not quietly turn a feature-gated call into an
  always-on one; `runDetection` still checks `config.sensitivityAwareness`.

## What was NOT built (A2), and why

The plan called for the seriousness judge to join the batch speculatively — launch it alongside the
other two and discard its verdict when a disclosure turns up. Worth another ~371 ms/turn.

**It was not built, because the plan had misread the guarantee it would break.**

The plan assumed the safeguarding invariant was about the outcome: _a disclosure is never struck_.
Under that reading, speculating is fine as long as the verdict is thrown away. Reading the tests
said otherwise. Three of them assert `calls.serious` is **zero** on a disclosure turn, one commented
_"protected: neither struck nor judged"_. The guarantee is that a disclosure is never sent to the
sincerity judge **at all**.

That cannot survive speculation, because whether this turn carries a disclosure is unknowable until
the other two calls return, and any of three signals can be the one that flags it:

| Signal                                  | Known before the batch? |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| deterministic keyword floor             | yes — could be skipped  |
| the dedicated detector                  | **no**                  |
| the extractor's own `sensitivity` field | **no**                  |

There is no configuration where speculation is provably safe: the extractor's field gates the judge
even when the sensitivity feature is switched off entirely.

Proven rather than argued — adding the judge to the batch fails **ten** tests across both
orchestrators, three of them the safeguarding guarantees:

```
× keeps the seriousness judge OUT of the concurrent batch
× SAFEGUARDING OUTRANKS THE SINCERITY GATE: a detected disclosure is never … struck …
× DETECTS via the dedicated detector even when the extractor MISSED the disclosure (the bug)
… and five more
```

So A2 is a values decision, not an engineering one, and it is recorded in the plan for the owner
rather than taken here. The recommendation there is to leave it: ~371 ms is ~7% of a turn, and
Phase 4 targets a stage worth 46% of it.

## A consequence worth remembering

**The Diagnostics residual is now a floor rather than a measurement**, exactly as
[`F20.1`](./f20.1.md) warned it would become. `residualMs` is `wall-clock − Σ call latency`, which
was meaningful only while every call ran in sequence. With two overlapping, the summed latency can
exceed the wall-clock and the residual clamps to 0. That still reads honestly — the turn was
model-bound throughout — but it can no longer be read as "there is no overhead". The clamp is
tested; the caveat is on the type and in
[`diagnostics.md`](../questionnaire/diagnostics.md#where-the-time-goes-stage-latency).

## Tests

Eight added; the two that matter are deadlock-shaped and mutation-verified.

- **Overlap (2, one per orchestrator).** Proven by deliberate deadlock: extraction cannot resolve
  until detection has _started_. Re-serialise the code and the test times out rather than passing
  silently, which is the failure mode a naive "both were called" assertion would miss entirely.
- **The judge stays out of the batch (2).** A slow detector that reports a disclosure: if the judge
  had been launched alongside, it would have run before the disclosure was known. Asserts it did
  not.
- **What concurrency must not change (4).** `toolCall` order when the calls finish out of order,
  cost summed either way, the detector still skipped when the feature is off, and the data-slot
  pipeline's `dataSlotFills` still carried through.

The 175 pre-existing orchestrator tests passed unchanged, which is the main evidence that behaviour
did not move.
