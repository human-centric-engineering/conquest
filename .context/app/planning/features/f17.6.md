# F17.6 — Respondent amendment

> Answers open question 7 of the pilot client research: _"The respondent is never asked which sections to
> run" is unambiguous for the selection. It says nothing about whether someone who says "actually,
> ask me about talent" mid-run should be honoured._ Recommendation, now implemented: honour it,
> record it, and keep it out of routing-quality analytics.

## The tension being resolved

The plan is decided **once**, from the opening, and never recomputed. That is load-bearing: a plan
that shifted under a running interview would make a finished report unreproducible. But it means a
respondent whose opening under-sold something has no way to correct it, and the instrument's own
transparency guarantees them the chance — the announcement exists precisely so they _can_ object
before the time is spent.

## Confirmed decisions

- **It only ever adds.** An amendment brings an excluded conditional topic into scope; it can never
  remove one. A respondent declining a topic the instrument requires is a different feature with
  different consequences — partial scoring, an incomparable cohort — and quietly allowing it here
  would make every completed assessment mean something slightly different.

- **Three tiers, and the first two are free.** A regex **cue gate** rules out the overwhelming
  majority of turns before any query runs — nearly every respondent message is an answer, not a
  request. A deterministic **label match** resolves "ask me about talent" against a topic named
  "Talent". Only what survives both reaches a small **routing-tier** model call.

- **The cue list is deliberately narrow.** A false negative costs one unasked topic the respondent
  can ask for again in different words. A false positive spends a model call on every turn containing
  "about" — and, worse, risks widening an interview because someone mentioned a subject in passing.
  `"Talent is our biggest problem"` is an **answer**, and matching it would fire on nearly every turn
  of an interview that is going well. Requests are phrased as requests, so the cues require the asking
  verb.

- **An ambiguous label match is not a match.** Two topics whose labels both appear in the message is
  exactly the case that needs judgement; picking the first would be a coin toss dressed as a decision.

- **The routing tier, not reasoning.** This is a short classification over a handful of labels, and it
  runs while a respondent waits. The planner's own judgement call is the expensive one.

- **A hallucinated key resolves to no amendment**, never to the nearest topic. "Close enough" here
  means asking someone about something they did not raise.

- **The added topic runs at `full` depth**, whatever the topic's own depth says. Someone who asks to
  be asked about something is asking to be _assessed_ on it; answering with a two-question sample
  would be a worse response than the exclusion they objected to.

- **Recorded twice, on purpose.** `InterviewPlan.amendments` carries when and why; the topic entry
  carries `source: 'respondent'`. Routing analytics must be able to tell a correction from a planner
  success — **a correction is evidence _about_ the planner, not an example of it working**, and
  counting an amended topic as a good selection would make the planner look better the worse it got.

- **`amendments` is omitted when empty**, never stored as `[]`, so "nobody amended this plan" and
  "this plan predates amendments" stay indistinguishable — as they should, since neither says
  anything about the planner.

- **The original announcement and `decidedAtTurn` are never rewritten.** The plan's record of what it
  decided, and what the respondent was told at the time, is what makes a finished report defensible.
  An amendment adds to it.

- **The acknowledgement rides the same briefing seam, matched on `atTurn`.** One outing, exactly as
  `decidedAtTurn` gives the original announcement — otherwise the interviewer re-acknowledges the
  request every turn for the rest of the interview.

- **Never throws.** Every failure leaves the plan as it was; the request goes unhonoured, which is
  the same outcome as the feature being off, and the respondent can ask again. A turn that 500s
  because an optional courtesy failed is not a trade worth making.

## Follow-ups

- **The Topics settings card exposes the switch but nothing reports on it.** There is no admin view
  of "how often respondents corrected the plan", which is the single most useful signal about whether
  a version's criteria are wrong. The data is on the plan and in `AppAiRun`; nothing reads it yet.
- **The cue gate is English-only.** A localised respondent surface would need the cue list per
  locale, or the gate replaced by a cheap classifier.
