# F20.5 — A wait you can actually read

> [`F20.2`](./f20.2.md) made the wait honest. It did not make it legible: the labels were true, in
> order, and sometimes gone before anyone could read them.

## What was wrong

Nothing about the stages was wrong — the reporting was. The pipeline crosses its boundaries at its
own pace: `reading` covers three model calls and can be gone in a few hundred milliseconds, and
`choosing` → `composing` is frequently back-to-back. Streamed straight to the surface and swapped in
place, two or three sentences flash past inside a second. The respondent registers motion and reads
none of it, which is arguably worse than the single static `Thinking…` the feature replaced.

Reported from use: _"these are sometimes replaced too rapidly."_

## What it does

Two presentation-only pieces. **No stage is invented, delayed at the start of a turn, re-ordered or
dropped** — the account of the wait is exactly the one F20.2 streams.

- **A dwell.** `usePacedStageLabel` holds each label for `STAGE_MIN_DWELL_MS` (1.1s) and **queues**
  whatever arrives during it. 1.1s is a comfortable read of the longest label ("Checking that against
  what you've told me…"), and four of them still fit inside the ~5s turn F20.1 measured.
- **A fade in place.** `TurnProgress` keeps one row: a label fades out over 180ms, is replaced while
  invisible, and fades back in. Out, then in — never both at once, and never with a slide.

## The decisions worth keeping

- **Paced once, in `ConversationProvider`.** Two surfaces read this label — the transcript's
  indicator and the composer's disabled-field placeholder. Paced in each consumer instead, the two
  clocks start at different moments and drift within a turn, so the composer names a stage the
  transcript has not reached. The stream hook still exposes the label raw; the provider is where it
  becomes the thing both halves show.
- **The first label of a turn is never held back.** The dwell exists to stop labels overwriting each
  other, not to sit on the neutral opener after the server has said what it is doing.
- **A turn that finishes early stops mid-sequence.** The queue is never drained on the way out, and
  a null label wipes it rather than flushing it. A stage announced after the reply has started — or
  beside the next turn's wait — would be the indicator describing finished work.
- **One row.** The first build of this scrolled the outgoing label away above the live one, keeping
  both readable. Two corrections came back from use: it was too much happening at once for a wait
  cue, and the taller box broke the turn's alignment — `AssistantTurn` pins the interviewer's accent
  mark to the FIRST line of a turn, so a two-row indicator left the mark floating a row above the
  words. The row now holds a `min-h-6` floor so its height never depends on the label.
- **Out then in, not a cross-fade.** Two labels at half opacity over each other in one row is a
  smear; `shown` lags the prop by one fade so the old words are gone before the new ones arrive.
- **Only the words change.** The dots and the clock hold still — furniture that moves for a change
  it had no part in is the fidget this set out to remove.
- **A label that has not changed does not fade.** The stream re-renders for reasons unrelated to the
  stage, and fading on every render would flicker continuously.
- **The animation is the small half.** What makes a fast sequence readable is the dwell; the fade
  only softens the change the dwell schedules.
- **Timing and drawing are split.** The hook decides WHEN a label changes, the component decides how
  that LOOKS. Which is why the component's tests never touch timing and the hook's never touch the
  DOM.

## Accessibility

Unchanged, deliberately: one live region, announcing the label and not the clock. Reduced motion
swaps the words outright rather than fading over zero milliseconds.

## Tests

12 added.

- **The hook (8).** The properties a careless rewrite loses: a burst queues rather than dropping,
  order survives, a repeat does not cost the next stage an extra dwell, a turn ending wipes the
  queue instead of draining it, the next turn does not inherit the last one's dwell, and the timer
  is cleared on unmount.
- **The component (4).** The outgoing words still on the row and transparent mid-fade (painting the
  new label there is the swap-under-the-eye this replaced), the new ones only after it, the row
  staying single and floored at `min-h-6`, and no fade at all for a re-render that did not change
  the stage.

## Not verified

Not eyeballed in a browser. The dwell, the queue, the trail's content and the re-keying are covered
by tests; how the fade _feels_ at 1.1s / 1.3s is a judgement call the numbers cannot make, and the
two constants are one edit apart.
